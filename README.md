# WoobleDex 📖

*All 1025 Pokémon in FireRed, LeafGreen, Ruby, Sapphire and Emerald, and in Red, Blue, Yellow,
Gold, Silver and Crystal. Catch, train, evolve and battle with every species up to Pecharunt.*

</div>

A mod for [Gen1Recomp](https://github.com/bryanthaboi/gen1recomp). The Generation 3 games know
386 Pokémon; this mod adds the other 639, so Sinnoh, Unova, Kalos, Alola, Galar and Paldea
Pokémon can live in Kanto and Hoenn. It does the same for the Generation 1 and 2 games, which
know 151 and 251 (see [On Red, Blue, Yellow, Gold, Silver and Crystal](#-on-red-blue-yellow-gold-silver-and-crystal)).

## ✨ Features

Core Mechanics and Fits the Game are FireRed, LeafGreen, Ruby, Sapphire and Emerald; Red to
Crystal have their own section after them.

### Core Mechanics

- 🆕 **639 new Pokémon** (#387–1025) that work like any other: catch, train, evolve, trade and
  battle with them. Each has its stats, typing, Pokédex entry and cry.
- 🔄 **New evolutions for old favourites:** Rhydon into Rhyperior, Magneton into Magnezone,
  Nosepass into Probopass, Scyther into Kleavor, Togetic into Togekiss and more.
- 💎 **The items to do it:** Dusk Stone, Protector, Dubious Disc and 36 more evolution items,
  all sold by a vending machine in the department store. Each unlocks with the Gym Badges it
  matches in the modern games: Dusk Stone and Linking Cord early, Ice Stone and Razor Claw at 4,
  Metal Alloy at 8.
- 🏪 **Vending machines:** two touch-screen machines, EVO EXPRESS for the evolution items and TM
  EXPRESS for the new TMs: in the Celadon Dept. Store (EVO EXPRESS on 4F, TM EXPRESS on 2F) in
  FireRed / LeafGreen, and the Lilycove Dept. Store (EVO EXPRESS on 2F, TM EXPRESS on 4F) in Ruby /
  Sapphire / Emerald. Browse by tab (stones, trade
  items, held items...; TMs by move type), see each item's description and a TM's type, power,
  accuracy and PP before you buy, pick how many, and watch it drop into the tray. Works with the
  buttons alone (the D-pad reaches the tabs too) or with touch and the mouse, and the screen fills
  your window, wide or held upright. Each machine boots up with its own logo first (a tap or any
  button skips it).
- 🔗 **Linking Cord:** use it on a Pokémon that evolves by trading and it evolves on the spot, no
  trade partner needed: Kadabra, Machoke, Boldore and the rest, and the ones that trade holding an
  item too (Porygon2 holding a Dubious Disc, Onix holding a Metal Coat), which use the item up just
  as a trade would. Sold beside the evolution stones.
- 🍬 **Exp. Candies XS to XL:** use one on a Pokémon from the Bag for a boost of Exp. Points, with
  its level-ups, new moves and evolutions just as a Rare Candy gives them. The base games don't
  hand them out; Dynamax Raids gives them as raid rewards.
- 🔴 **Dynamax Levels and the Gigantamax Factor:** every Pokémon has a Dynamax Level (0–10) and can
  carry the Gigantamax Factor, shown on its summary - a Dynamax Level gauge on the info page, with the
  Dynamax icon beside it when it has the factor - and the icon in the PC too. **Dynamax Candy** raises
  the level; three **Max Mushrooms** make a Max Soup that gives a Pokémon with a Gigantamax form the
  factor, or takes it away (Urshifu's takes a **Max Honey** too). All three are sold beside the
  vitamins. Battles don't Dynamax here: Dynamax Raids uses them in its raids, gives Dynamax Candy and the
  marks on what you catch there, and takes a **Dynamax Crystal** at a den for a legendary Pokémon's
  raid.
- 🌍 **128 alternate forms:** Alolan, Galarian, Hisuian and Paldean forms (of the originals too,
  each evolving into its own region's form), Rotom's appliances, Wormadam's cloaks,
  Oricorio's styles, Alcremie's creams, Giratina Origin, Kyurem Black and many more, each a
  Pokémon of its own.

### Fits the Game

- ⚔️ **419 newer moves, for real:** Moonblast, Dazzling Gleam, Play Rough, Shadow Claw, Earth
  Power, Flash Cannon, Brave Bird, Aqua Jet and more, with their own power, accuracy, PP, priority,
  type, battle text and description, including new mechanics: Nasty Plot, Quiver Dance, Close
  Combat, Flare Blitz, Draining Kiss, Shore Up, and moves that read the battle — Hex, Venoshock,
  Brine, Acrobatics, Payback, Stored Power, Heavy Slam, Gyro Ball, Foul Play, Body Press,
  Psyshock, Sucker Punch, First Impression, Final Gambit, Wicked Blow, Fell Stinger, Mind Blown
  and more.
- 🎬 **Animations to match:** 134 newer moves play their modern-style animation (Moonblast calls
  down the moon, Psychic Fangs bites through a psychic haze), built from Gen 3's own graphics.
  20 more get effects drawn just for them: Dragon Pulse and Flash Cannon fire a beam, Aura
  Sphere and Focus Blast hurl a glowing orb that bursts, Stone Edge drives a spike up from the
  ground, Hurricane whips up a twister, Bolt Strike calls down lightning. The rest borrow the
  closest Gen 3 animation (Force Palm strikes like Karate Chop).
- 💿 **114 new TMs (TM51–TM164):** every newer move that's a TM in today's games, from Dazzling
  Gleam and Earth Power to Scald, Stealth Rock and U-turn. Buy them from the TM vending machine:
  Celadon Dept. Store 2F (FireRed / LeafGreen) or Lilycove Dept. Store 4F (Ruby / Sapphire /
  Emerald). Each unlocks with the Gym Badges it matches in the modern games (Stealth Rock and
  Tailwind from the start, Volt Switch at 3, Scald and Close Combat at 8); the TM Case
  holds them all, and a Pokémon can learn one if it learns that move by TM in the modern games.
  They follow Scarlet / Violet's TM order after Gen 3's own 50 (Fire Fang is TM51), with the
  Sword / Shield-only ones at the end.
- 🌋 **The battlefield itself:** Stealth Rock, Toxic Spikes and Sticky Web wait for whatever
  switches in; Tailwind and Trick Room change who moves first; Electric, Grassy, Misty and
  Psychic Terrain cover the field for five turns; U-turn, Volt Switch, Flip Turn and Parting
  Shot hit and bring in a teammate; Dragon Tail and Circle Throw throw the foe out; King's
  Shield, Spiky Shield and Baneful Bunker punish whoever touches them; Gravity, Magnet Rise,
  Wonder Room, Magic Room, Aurora Veil, Healing Wish, Soak, Worry Seed, Power Split, Heart Swap,
  Fling and Natural Gift all do what they do today. Seven moves that need a partner to act out
  of turn (After You, Quash, Instruct, Me First, Ally Switch, Bestow, Teatime) are swapped, at
  the same level, for the closest Gen 3 move of the same type.
- 🧬 **52 newer abilities, for real:** Technician, Adaptability, Multiscale, Snow Warning,
  Regenerator, Prankster, Moxie, Sap Sipper, Iron Fist, Strong Jaw, Frisk and more work in
  battle, with their own name and description. Pokémon you already have get them too. Every
  other newer ability is the closest Gen 3 one.
- 🧚 **The Fairy type:** Fairy is a real type, with today's matchups (it beats Dragon, Dark and
  Fighting; Dragon can't touch it; Poison and Steel hit it hard) and its own type icon. Every
  Pokémon has its modern typing, the originals too: Clefairy, Jigglypuff, Togepi, Snubbull,
  Marill, Mr. Mime, Ralts and Mawile are part Fairy, and Charm, Sweet Kiss and Moonlight are
  Fairy moves. Steel no longer resists Ghost and Dark, as in today's games.
- 🏷️ **Type badges in battle:** small type badges sit beside each HP box, to the right of the
  foe's and to the left of yours, so you can read a matchup at a glance. They follow the battle:
  a Pokémon hit by Soak, or using Roost or Transform, shows its new types. They stay beside their
  box in singles and doubles, and with WoobleView's Fill Screen.
- 📦 **A bigger PC:** 40 boxes, room for 1,200 Pokémon, so every species and form fits with
  space to spare. The extra boxes use the game's own wallpapers and work everywhere the first
  14 do: switching boxes, depositing, catching straight into the PC and the save editor.
- 🌅 **Day and night:** the outdoors follows the clock. Mornings (4–10) are soft and hazy,
  afternoons (10–17) are the game's own look, evenings (17–20) glow warm orange and nights
  (20–4) turn a moonlit blue, dim enough to feel like night but bright enough to play. It
  changes when you enter an area; houses, caves, battles and menus keep their usual colors.
  Ruby, Sapphire and Emerald use the in-game clock (or your device's, see Clock Source), FireRed
  and LeafGreen your device's.
- 📖 **A bigger Pokédex:** The new Pokémon join the National Pokédex, with a FORMS page for
  species with alternate forms, and fast scrolling when you hold a direction.

### 🕹️ On Red, Blue, Yellow, Gold, Silver and Crystal

- 🆕 **Every Pokémon the game doesn't have:** #152–1025 in Red, Blue and Yellow, #252–1025 in
  Gold, Silver and Crystal, plus the 128 alternate forms. Each has its stats, modern typing,
  level-up moves, TM moves, Pokédex entry (fitted to the game's own page) and a party icon.
- 🧚 **Modern types:** Fairy joins both games, and Dark and Steel join Red, Blue and Yellow,
  with today's matchups. The originals get their modern typing too: Magnemite is Electric /
  Steel, Clefairy is Fairy, Bite is a Dark move.
- ⚔️ **Every newer move, for real, in the game's own battles:** all of them work with today's
  effects, each with the look of a similar move: Rage Fist grows with every hit taken, Stealth
  Rock and Spikes hurt whoever comes in, U-turn switches you out, Protect and King's Shield
  block, Trick Room flips the turn order, Knock Off swats an item away, Skill Swap trades
  abilities. Only the moves that need a partner beside you (Helping Hand, Follow Me, After
  You...) are swapped for a similar one, as in the Generation 3 games.
- 🌦️ **Weather and terrain everywhere:** rain, sun, sandstorm, hail and snow, Electric, Grassy,
  Misty and Psychic Terrain, with their modern effects (Gold, Silver and Crystal keep their own
  rain, sun and sandstorm).
- 💪 **Abilities:** every Pokémon has one of its species' abilities, the same one for good:
  Intimidate, Levitate, Swift Swim, Water Absorb, Static, Sturdy, Guts, Technician and many more
  do what they do today.
- 🌿 **Natures:** every Pokémon has a nature that raises one stat and lowers another, as in the
  newer games (in Red, Blue and Yellow, Sp. Atk and Sp. Def natures move the one SPECIAL).
- 📊 **The summary shows it all:** nature, ability and held item on a third page in Red, Blue and
  Yellow, and under OT on the stats page in Gold, Silver and Crystal.
- ✨ **Their own animations:** Aura Sphere, Dark Pulse, Dragon Pulse, Flash Cannon, Focus Blast,
  Night Slash, Stone Edge and more (18 in all) play effects of their own instead of an older
  move's.
- 🔄 **Evolutions:** by level, stone, trade, friendship, time of day, held item, gender, a known
  move, a party member, steps walked and more, and the originals' newer evolutions too (Golbat
  into Crobat in Red, Eevee into Leafeon, Glaceon and Sylveon). Red, Blue and Yellow keep a
  hidden friendship for every Pokémon, which grows as it levels up in battle and walks with you.
- 💎 **The items to do it:** the evolution items each game lacks, sold by the EVO EXPRESS
  vending machine (below). In Red, Blue and Yellow, an item an evolution needs held or traded
  is used on the Pokémon like a stone (a Metal Coat turns Onix into Steelix).
- 🎒 **Held items in Red, Blue and Yellow:** Gold's held items (berries, Leftovers, King's Rock,
  Quick Claw, Scope Lens, the type boosters, Light Ball, Everstone, Smoke Ball...) work in
  battle, sold at the Celadon Dept. Store 4F. Give or take them with ITEM on the party menu.
- ♂️ **Gender in Red, Blue and Yellow:** every Pokémon is male, female or neither, by Gold's own
  rule, shown after its level in battle.
- 🥚 **Breeding in Red, Blue and Yellow:** the Day Care Lady, beside the gentleman in the Route 5
  Day Care, raises a second Pokémon. Two that get along leave an Egg with her, which you carry
  until it hatches into the party, with any egg moves its parents know.
- 🏪 **Vending machines:** the same touch-screen machines as in the Generation 3 games: EVO
  EXPRESS for the evolution items and TM EXPRESS for the new TMs, in the Celadon Dept. Store
  (EVO EXPRESS on 4F, TM EXPRESS on 2F) in Red, Blue and Yellow and the Goldenrod Dept. Store
  (EVO EXPRESS on 4F, TM EXPRESS on 5F) in Gold, Silver and Crystal. Browse by tab, see each
  TM's move, type and power, buy several at once; some items wait until you have enough Gym
  Badges.
- 🔊 **Cries:** #387–1025 and their forms have their own cry; the rest borrow a similar
  Pokémon's.
- 📖 **The Pokédex** runs to #1025, with four-digit numbers, and a FORMS page for a species
  with alternate forms (Vulpix's Alolan form, Alcremie's creams...): in Red, Blue and Yellow, A
  on an entry's last page; in Gold, Silver and Crystal, the third PAGE. Up and Down step through
  the forms.
- 🔗 **Linking Cord:** use it on a Pokémon that evolves by trading and it evolves on the spot
  (Kadabra, Machoke, Boldore...). In Gold, Silver and Crystal, one that trades holding an item
  must hold it, and uses it up, just as in a trade. Sold by EVO EXPRESS.
- 🆙 **Modern Moves:** the game's own Pokémon also learn the newer moves they learn today, at the
  same levels, plus newer egg moves in Gold, Silver and Crystal (only moves the game can do).
- 🌅 **Day and night in Red, Blue and Yellow:** the outdoors follows your device's clock, the same
  morning, evening and night looks as in the Generation 3 games. Gold, Silver and Crystal keep
  their own day and night.
- 📋 **Register Owned:** opening the Pokédex counts every Pokémon in your party and PC.
- 💿 **114 new TMs:** TM51 to TM164, the same as in the Generation 3 games (Zen Headbutt,
  Stealth Rock, U-turn, Scald, Dazzling Gleam...), sold by TM EXPRESS. They teach like
  the game's own TMs, with ABLE / NOT ABLE on the party screen, and in Gold, Silver and Crystal
  they sit in the TM pocket between TM50 and the HMs.
- 🍬 **Exp. Candies XS to XL:** 100 to 30,000 Exp. Points from the Bag, sold with the vitamins
  (Celadon Dept. Store 5F; Goldenrod Dept. Store 4F). Like a Rare Candy, each level shows its
  stats, offers every move learned on the way and can set off an evolution.
- 🏷️ **Type badges:** small badges beside each HP box in battle show the Pokémon's types, as on
  the Gen 3 games: to the right of the foe's name and HP bar, to the left of yours. They stay
  beside their box when the battle fills the window.
- 📦 **Bigger PC:** 40 boxes (800 Pokémon) instead of 12 or 14.

## ⚙️ Options

Open **OPTIONS → MODS → WoobleDex**. Each option is one setting for every game it appears in.
Red to Crystal show Modern Moves, Register Owned, Bigger PC, Type Badges and Move Split, and
Red, Blue and Yellow also Day & Night; the rest are for FireRed to Emerald.

| Option | Choices | Default | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Clock Source** | In-game / Device | In-game | Which clock Day & Night and the time-of-day evolutions read (Midnight Lycanroc, Alcremie's night and day creams, Budew, Riolu, Chingling, Snom). FireRed and LeafGreen have no clock, so they always use the device's. |
| **National Dex** | On / Off | Off | Turns the National Pokédex on from the start, instead of earning it in the story. Nothing is written to your save. |
| **Day & Night** | On / Off | On | Tints the outdoors for morning, evening and night; the afternoon is the game's own look. Changes when you enter an area. Indoors, caves, battles and menus are never tinted. Red, Blue and Yellow use the device's clock; Gold, Silver and Crystal have their own day and night instead. |
| **Modern Moves** | On / Off | On | The game's own Pokémon also learn the newer moves they learn today, at the same levels (Clefairy learns Moonblast at 44), plus newer egg moves. Their own moves stay, and moves they already know are kept if you turn it off. On Red to Crystal only moves the game can do are added. The save editor follows this setting. |
| **Bigger PC** | On / Off | On | 40 PC boxes instead of the game's own: 14 in FireRed to Emerald (1,200 Pokémon), 12 in Red, Blue and Yellow and 14 in Gold, Silver and Crystal (800 Pokémon). Turning it off hides the extra boxes; the Pokémon in them stay in your save and come back when you turn it on again. |
| **Register Owned** | On / Off | On | Opening the Pokédex counts every Pokémon in your party and PC as seen and caught, handy for Pokémon added with a save editor. |
| **Type Badges** | On / Off | On | Shows each Pokémon's types as small badges by its HP box in battle. |
| **Move Split** | On / Off | Off | Red to Crystal: a move is physical or special by the move itself, as in today's games (Shadow Ball special; Crunch and Fire Punch physical), instead of by its type. It changes how the game's own moves hit too. |

## 💾 Installation

1. Import your own FireRed, LeafGreen, Ruby, Sapphire or Emerald (or Red, Blue, Yellow, Gold,
   Silver or Crystal) into [Gen1Recomp](https://github.com/bryanthaboi/gen1recomp) **0.3.95 or
   newer** (older versions can't hold this many Pokémon; on those, keep version 1.1.4).
2. Download the latest `national_dex_gen3-….zip` from the
   [Releases](https://github.com/poooooby/national_dex_gen3/releases/latest) page.
3. In the game, go to **MODS → Import mod .zip** and choose the file.
4. Add the companion mods below and make sure all of them are on.

## 🔗 Works With

This mod adds the Pokémon. Two companions bring them to life:

- 🎞️ [WoobleSpritePack](https://github.com/poooooby/g9-battle-sprites-gen3): **the
  pictures.** Animated battle sprites, summary and Pokédex pictures and party icons. Without it,
  new Pokémon show a blank or garbled picture. On Red to Crystal the new Pokémon show a grey "?"
  ball instead, until a sprite mod draws them.
- 🗺️ [WoobleSpawns](https://github.com/poooooby/g1r_modern_spawns): **the wild.** Fills every
  area with Pokémon from all generations, on every game. With it, the Pokédex can even show where
  to find a species you haven't seen yet.
- 🌿 [Wilds of Hoenn](https://github.com/poooooby/wilds-of-hoenn): see them roaming the overworld,
  each with its own sprite.

It also plays well with [Event Distributions](https://github.com/CapnJames95/gen1recomp-event-distributions):
the game's own Pokémon keep their original data, so its event gifts arrive as usual.

## 📌 Good to Know

- **WoobleDex is the mod once called National Dex Gen 3.** It updates in place: your saves,
  settings and every mod made for it keep working.
- **Forms can't be found in the wild yet.** Gen 3 can't change a Pokémon's form, so they come
  from a save editor, plus a few that evolve into each other. Megas and Gigantamax forms aren't
  included.
- **Some evolutions are adapted.** Conditions Gen 3 can't check (a known move, rain, a held item
  at a time of day ...) get stand-ins, e.g. Piloswine → Mamoswine while knowing Rock Tomb, and
  Galarian Farfetch'd → Sirfetch'd after 3 critical hits in one battle.
- **Newer moves** are learned by level-up, as egg moves and from the new TMs; move tutors are
  Gen 3's own. The Move Reminder offers every level-up move up to a Pokémon's level, however
  long its list. The new TMs don't pass on to eggs from the father, as Gen 3's TMs do.
- **Boxes 15–40 are kept in this mod's part of your save,** so the save stays a normal 14-box
  save underneath: turning Bigger PC off or removing the mod never loses them. Tools that only
  know the 14 boxes see boxes 1–14: exporting to a cartridge .sav, the cross-game Pokémon Box,
  online team picking, and Ruby/Sapphire's lottery and PokéNav condition search.
- **Exporting to a cartridge .sav** leaves Hall of Fame entries for Pokémon from #448 on (and
  the alternate forms) out: the cartridge format can't store them. The game says so when it
  exports; your save itself keeps them.
- **Ruby and Sapphire's Bag** squeezes the three-digit numbers of TM100 and up against the move
  name; FireRed, LeafGreen and Emerald show them in full.
- **The original 386 Pokémon,** the story, trainers and maps are unchanged, apart from the
  originals' Fairy typing, their new evolutions and, with **Modern Moves** on, their newer moves.
- **A few abilities work in part.** Leaf Guard, Sweet Veil and Flower Veil stop status moves but
  not a status an attack can cause; Super Luck doesn't stack with a high critical-hit move;
  Ice Body and Snow Cloak don't block hail damage.
- **Moves follow Gen 3's rules.** A move's type decides whether it is physical or special (Fairy
  moves are special, Shadow Claw is physical; Foul Play, a Dark move, turns the foe's Attack
  against it as a special hit), and contest data for the newest moves is basic.
  They are learned by level-up and as egg moves; TMs and tutors are the original ones.
- **The save editor** that comes with gen1recomp knows everything this mod adds: every Pokémon
  and form, their items and moves, Fairy typing and the newer abilities.
- **Removing this mod** from a save whose Pokémon know its moves or are its species breaks those
  Pokémon; keep the mod installed for that save.
- **Link play** expects both players to have the same type chart; trade and battle with
  players who use this mod too.

### On Red, Blue, Yellow, Gold, Silver and Crystal

- **It takes the place of the original National Dex mod** there: the two can't be on together.
  With both on, WoobleDex steps aside, so turn National Dex off for those games.
- **Battle mods made for the original National Dex** (the g9 battle mods, Battle Forms) ask for
  it by name, so for now they don't run beside WoobleDex. WoobleDex already answers everything
  they ask of a National Dex, so they can run on it once they also accept WoobleDex.
- **Red to Crystal's options** are Modern Moves, Register Owned, Bigger PC, Type Badges, Move
  Split and (Red, Blue, Yellow) Day & Night, shared with the Generation 3 games: turning one off
  turns it off everywhere.
- **The new TMs keep their Generation 3 numbers,** so a number is the same TM in every game (TM74
  is Zen Headbutt).
- **A Pokémon's nature and ability come from its stats' hidden values,** so they never change,
  and a Pokémon you already had gets them too (its stats are worked out again when you load).
- **A switch made by a move** (U-turn, Baton Pass, Dragon Tail) brings in the first healthy
  Pokémon in the party, as these games' own Baton Pass does.
- **The Egg in Red, Blue and Yellow is carried, not shown:** those games' menus have no Egg, so
  it waits with you until it hatches (one at a time).
- **Keep the mod installed on a Gold, Silver or Crystal save** that holds its Pokémon: those
  games have nowhere to set aside a Pokémon they don't know.
- **Exporting to a cartridge .sav** can't store the new Pokémon, and a newer move comes out as an
  empty move slot. In Red, Blue and Yellow, which have no Steel or Fairy, a Magnemite's Steel
  half comes out as Normal. Red, Blue and Yellow leave the new items out of the export; Gold,
  Silver and Crystal can't export a save that carries any (the new TMs are left out). Boxes past
  the game's own 12 or 14, held items and Eggs in Red, Blue and Yellow, and the second Day Care
  Pokémon stay behind in the mod's part of the save.
- **Some evolutions are adapted:** there is no overworld rain, so Sliggoo evolves at level 50;
  no battle ground, so a female Burmy becomes Plant Wormadam; recoil isn't counted, so
  White-Striped Basculin evolves at level 36.
- **Friendship in Red, Blue and Yellow** grows only with level-ups in battle or from Exp.
  Candies and with walking, not with vitamins or Rare Candies.
- **Gold, Silver and Crystal's summary** prints a number from #1000 on right against the level
  ("No.1000:L50").
- **In Gold, Silver and Crystal, the new Pokémon turn grey while a move animation plays,** and
  get their colours back when it ends: the game colours a Pokémon during an animation from its
  own palette list, which they aren't in.
